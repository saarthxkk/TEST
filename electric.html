Ohm's Law: Current (I) = Voltage (V) ÷ Resistance (R). When resistance increases, the current decreases and the bulb glows dimmer. When resistance decreases, more current flows and the bulb glows brighter!

HEATING EFFECT

Electricity Makes Things Hot

See how electric current heats up a nichrome wire. The more current flows, the hotter and brighter it gets!

1A
TEMPERATURE 25°C
💡

Electric Bulb

Filament heats up to ~2500°C and glows white-hot, producing light.

🔌

Electric Iron

Nichrome coil heats up when current flows. Used for pressing clothes.

⚡

Electric Fuse

Safety device — melts and breaks circuit when too much current flows.

🍞

Electric Heater

Coil glows red-hot due to heating effect. Warms room or cooks food.

🔥
Heating Effect: When electric current flows through a conductor, it produces heat. The amount of heat depends on the current, resistance, and time. This is called Joule's law of heating: H = I²Rt
03
MAGNETIC EFFECT

Electricity Creates Magnets

Wrap wire around a nail and pass current — it becomes an electromagnet! Watch it attract iron objects.

10 turns
🧲
Electromagnet: When electric current flows through a coil of wire wound around an iron nail, it becomes a magnet. Increasing the number of turns or the current makes the electromagnet stronger. Electromagnets are used in cranes, doorbells, and MRI machines!

Electroplating & Electrolysis

🔬
Chemical Effect: When electric current passes through a conducting solution (electrolyte), chemical reactions occur. Metal gets deposited on the electrode — this process is called electroplating. It's used to coat iron with chromium (taps), gold/silver plating on jewelry, and tin coating on food cans!
